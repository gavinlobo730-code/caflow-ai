## 3. Strategy thesis (30 Sep 2026)

Plain answer first. PracticeSync can realistically become the preferred practice platform for one kind of firm (small CA firms with about 15 to 150 SME clients that keep their books in Tally today), starting in one city, over several years. It cannot realistically become the only product Indian CAs use, and trying to engineer that would put its one rare asset, a reputation for telling the truth, at risk. Every lens agreed on this. After the skeptic reviews, no strategy move is strong. Only three moats are even moderate: named reference firms in one region, verified statutory rules shown with their source, and multi-client payroll for firms that run payroll for many companies. Everything else is either a basic requirement every rival already has (Tally import, filing rails, AI reading of invoices, a privacy page) or needs volume the product does not have (about 7 live clients, no paying firm, no reference firm). The order of work follows from that. First, fix what misleads or silently costs money: a tax rate that changes when an invoice is re-saved, a 40% GST slab that cannot be picked, AI that turns an exempt 0% line into 18%, a 'Filed' button that does not lock the books, marketing that promises things the code does not do, a free-tier AI key receiving client documents, no proof of backups, and database access holes for staff who have been suspended. These are cheap and they protect credibility. Second, remove the walls a Tally-based CA hits first: a real Tally import with a check that totals match to the paisa (aimed at the 1 April 2027 new-year boundary), a way out (full export), and the client loop (portal upload, notifications, tasks from deadlines, one GST screen for many clients). Third, start early the registrations that take months even though they finish late: the free steps now, then rent the GST connection from an existing licensed provider, read-only first; the ITR file waits for the new-Act schemas. Fourth, let the moats accumulate: pilot firms and references, verified rules, payroll history, and the books-to-return-to-lock loop. Choices I made where the plans disagreed. (1) Positioning is 'works beside Tally' for the practice layer, and the books move client by client at a year boundary; a long side-by-side run of GST is not supported because returns are built from invoices and bills, not from the ledger. (2) Pricing starts as a founding-firm offer with written terms, annual prepay and all staff seats included; no public list price until Tally import and filing gaps close; first invoices are raised by hand from the product's own books. (3) Backups: Supabase Pro-level daily backups plus an independent copy of the database and uploaded files and a timed restore now; point-in-time recovery only once the database size and a paying firm justify it; moving compute to India is deferred, and the website wording is made exact instead. (4) Certifications (ISO 27001, SOC 2) wait for a named buyer; an independent penetration test comes first, after the holes are fixed. (5) Items the plans mark as already fixed (for example the first-click session error, duplicate-asset click guard, hard-reload 404s, invite acceptance, plain sign-up errors, the multi-GSTIN save check, the AI canned-text fix) are left out of the ranking and the roadmap; they remain in the per-dimension plans. (6) Strategy moves the skeptics dropped stay dropped: the student and articled-assistant pipeline, employee portability across employers, pooled cross-firm benchmarks, the GSP-counterparty scorecard, the open-sourced rule registry and the 'fair stickiness' recap. How the stage scores were estimated. I took the listed uplifts of the items finished in each stage, counted about 70% of them (the plans overlap and some fixes appear in two plans), scaled them down as a dimension nears its target, never went above the plan's 'if all done' target, and then held a dimension below the plan author's own proof condition until the exit criteria say that proof exists: a file actually passing the government's TDS file checker, an ITR file accepted by the portal, a full year of payroll, reference firms, an independent penetration test. The starting overall is 5.0 as rated (the eleven scores average 5.08). The end-of-window overall is about 7.1. These are estimates, not promises, and they assume one or two engineers working with AI help, the owner decisions listed under owner_unblocks arriving within weeks, and at least three pilot firms existing. If decisions slip, every later stage slips with them.

## 4. The 25 priorities, in order

1. **Make every public claim true, and say plainly what the product does not do yet** — effort: days; needs: owner decision on which promises to delete or rewrite. The site and some screens promise single sign-on, an SLA, two-factor on every sign-in, Tally ledgers and opening balances, and an ITR file 'ready to file'. The code does not do these (checked: the Tally importer writes customers and vendors only; two-factor covers Partner and Manager on some routes). A CA's partner checks security and capability sentences first, and one mismatch costs the whole page its credibility. The repo already has an honesty test for the marketing site, so extending it is cheap. The Resources page also quotes a wrong TDS deadline for Q4 (31 May, not 30 April).
2. **Stop the wrong-number bugs in the tax screens** — effort: hours to days; needs: none. Checked at HEAD: re-opening an invoice and saving can change a fractional GST rate (150 basis points reloads as 2%), the rate list has no 40% slab a year after it began, AI extraction turns a stated 0% into 18%, a return for a second registration is built from the whole client's documents and looks authoritative, and a year with unverified rates carries no written warning. Each one silently changes tax a client pays or claims. Most are hours of work.
3. **Make 'Filed' lock the period from every screen** — effort: days; needs: none. Two Mark Filed paths exist. The one on the Deadlines page walks the status but never records the filing, so the books for that month stay open while the screen shows a green tick. The GST tracker path is correct. One small fix aligns both and protects the product's ledger-to-return tie-out.
4. **Take the AI path off unsafe defaults and prove it works live** — effort: days; needs: money (paid AI terms) and owner decision (per-firm AI switch). Client invoice photos and scanned bank statements go to a Gemini key the code describes as free-tier with no billing. As recalled (not verified here), Google's unpaid terms allow submitted content to be used to improve its products. A second copilot path still sends client PAN and GSTIN to Groq. No successful live call is recorded since the Groq model was replaced on 29 September. The executive dashboard shows numbers the software made up. Fixing this is cheap and is the precondition for any later AI feature.
5. **Always-on, backed up, and watched** — effort: hours to days; needs: money (paid API host, database plan, mail service) and owner decision (who is alerted). A measured cold start of 56.55 seconds is the first thing a CA notices in a demo. The repo cannot show which database plan or backup setting production has. The only alarm is a smoke test that runs every 4 to 9 hours in practice. Invites and sign-ups have already hit the shared email rate limit (27 September). Until these are fixed, a demo or a pilot can fail for reasons unrelated to the product.
6. **Close the four holes in the browser's direct database path** — effort: days each; needs: owner decision (two-factor policy for all staff or only some roles; who may read payroll). Per the review (reasoned from migrations, not run): suspending a staff member does not cut their access to the database and file storage; any assigned junior can read employees' salary, PAN and bank details straight from the browser; the document bucket is scoped by firm folder only, not by client assignment; and two-factor covers only some routers. Each is a days-sized fix with a real-Postgres test. A penetration test before these are fixed would only report them.
7. **Publish the legal and trust pack with counsel: privacy notice, terms, processor agreement, sub-processor list, incident plan** — effort: weeks (counsel is the slow part); needs: money (counsel) and owner decision (legal entity, grievance contact). The footer's Privacy and Terms links both open the support page. A CA firm is the data fiduciary for its clients and needs a processor agreement before it can lawfully load client data; the repo's own notes say this will become a sales gate and that the main duties start on 13 May 2027 (verified by search in the repo, not read from the Gazette). No real client data should enter the product until this exists.
8. **Back up for real, prove a restore, and rehearse database changes before they reach clients** — effort: weeks; needs: money (plan, staging copy, storage for the independent copy). Reported by the skeptic from Supabase's own documentation: its database backups do not include uploaded files, so a database-only restore proves half the story. Database changes merge straight into production with no rehearsal and are not applied all-or-nothing. A timed restore and a staging copy are the evidence a careful CA or their auditor will ask for.
9. **Engineering guardrails that stop the next silent mistake** — effort: days to weeks; needs: none (owner decision for required checks on the code host). There is no code linter, several web and upload libraries carry published security advisories (the review recalled them; a scanner should confirm), nothing scans dependencies or secrets, no test proves every firm-scoped query carries its firm filter, and the screen-walk robot exists but runs only by hand. The main design document is 375 KB and already states a wrong AI model default. These are days each and stop whole classes of error.
10. **Bring the statutory numbers up to today and say which are verified** — effort: days; needs: external data (a human reads Finance Act 2026, Income-tax Act 2025 rules, EPFO/ESIC and state notifications). FY 2026-27 slabs, payroll PF/ESI and several TDS rows are copies of 2025-26 flagged unverified, and every payslip and TDS deduction this year rests on them. Employer pension contributions may be over-taxed (80CCD(2)), one state's professional tax table is marked pending confirmation, and labour-code effects on gratuity, ESI and bonus are unsettled. A person has to read the primary documents, because this environment cannot reach government sites.
11. **Payroll gaps a real client meets in the first month** — effort: hours to weeks; needs: external data (state notifications, portal test uploads) and owner decision (Form 16 route, to be settled with a practising CA). The employee's Download payslip button calls a staff-only route. Mid-year joiners have no opening year-to-date position or previous-employer salary (only needed for clients who start mid-year; an April cutover needs none). Professional tax is modelled for 4 of about 22 states and welfare fund for none. Nothing delivers a Form 16 to the employee. None of the statutory files has been tried on the real portals.
12. **Bring a Tally client across in one sitting, and prove the totals match** — effort: months; needs: external data (real Tally exports, or Tally's free educational mode for test files) and owner decision (client-scoped account names, how prior-year comparatives are held, whether imported documents may exist without a ledger posting, whether mid-year cutover is supported). This is the first wall a Tally-based CA hits. The importer writes customers and vendors only (checked). The skeptic ran probes: a control-character reference in the file makes the parser return nothing while still saying it can proceed, and vouchers under another Tally tag produce no journals. Verified in the migrations: account names are unique per firm (migration 015), so importing each client's ledgers would collide without a decision and a migration. Returns are built from invoices and bills, so ledger-only import will not make GST work. Aim the cutover at 1 April 2027.
13. **A way out: whole-firm export in open formats and a written exit promise** — effort: weeks; needs: owner decision (exit wording, reviewed by counsel). A cautious CA asks what happens if the company shuts down. Only per-screen exports and year-end PDFs exist. Ship CSV, JSON, PDFs and the audit log first; a Tally voucher export is riskier (a bad file can damage a client's Tally) and waits for a real Tally import test. Easy exit also keeps the product on the right side of competition law.
14. **The GST month for a firm with 50 or more clients** — effort: weeks; needs: external data (a person tries the offline tool and commits the result). Today a CA pastes a 2B file into a text box per client, opens each client's tab, and sees a cash-to-pay figure that ignores credit carried forward. Since July 2025 the portal locks the outward tables of GSTR-3B to what was filed in GSTR-1, so a books-built 3B can differ from what the portal shows. One screen that shows every client's status and exceptions is the GST plan's main route to a competitive score. Also have a human load the generated return files into the real offline tool.
15. **Close the client loop: upload, notify, chase, turn deadlines into staged work** — effort: weeks; needs: owner decision (upload limits and scanning; opt-in automatic chasing). A portal client can see what was requested but cannot upload anything, new requests and messages notify nobody, four email functions have no caller, generated compliance obligations never become tasks, and time logged never becomes a correct bill. This loop is what a practice pays for every day. Automatic chasing was deliberately removed before; rebuild it only as an opt-in with a cap.
16. **Keyboard speed and consistent screens** — effort: weeks; needs: none. Tally users work with their hands on the keyboard: one key per voucher type, Enter to move between cells. Only the invoice grid has that flow today. A clerk who needs ten clicks per voucher will call the product slower whatever else it does better. A real-browser test for double-clicks on money screens is what catches the class of bug found on 29 September.
17. **A TDS file that passes the government's checker, real-file imports, and a filing-season workflow** — effort: weeks to months; needs: external data (a person downloads the current specification and checker from the government's TDS site, checks the licence for hosted use, and supplies real 26AS and Form 16 samples). Today the CA re-keys every TDS statement into the government utility. Q3 is due 31 Jan 2027 and Q4 on 31 May 2027. The design is to use the government's own file checker as the test, not to hand-write the format (it moved between versions). The 26AS reader cannot read the file as the portal issues it, Form 16 cannot be imported, and only two financial years of rates are held. Keep the claim at 'prepared, not filed' until a file has actually passed.
18. **Measure the AI, then widen it** — effort: weeks to months; needs: external data (labelled real documents with consent) and owner decision (assistant tools). There is no scoreboard of how often invoices are read correctly. Build a labelled test set (synthetic and public samples first, pilot documents only with consent), put every model call behind one gateway with limits and a fallback, run the existing statutory checks on AI output (GSTIN check digit, tax split against state, HSN as a number), and only then give the assistant read-only tools over the books. Tools that touch client figures must wait for paid terms, removal of identifiers from prompts, and scope checks.
19. **Run 3 to 5 pilot firms through real months, sign the tie-outs, and earn references** — effort: months; needs: owner decision (recruit firms, founding price, who runs weekly calls) and a named practising CA adviser. Nothing has been proven on real books beyond about 7 clients, and the trust score cannot pass about 6 without named firms that will take a reference call. Start with new small service clients beside Tally, then move willing clients at 1 April 2027. Bill the first firms by hand from the product's own books, at a founding price, rather than building subscription machinery first.
20. **Start the registrations that take months, then rent the GST connection read-only** — effort: months; needs: external registration and owner decision (reverse the 'no registrations' and prepare-only decisions for scoped API access only). Do the free, reversible steps early even though this ranks here: register as a third-party software developer on the income-tax portal, open the free e-invoice sandbox, and send the drafted enquiries. Then sign with an existing licensed provider for read-only GST access. The income-tax file build waits for the new-Act schemas, because the committed schemas serve returns for the year already filed. Filing itself (gst-29) is conditional on a pilot firm committing in writing and is not counted in the scores.
21. **Fill the ledger and statement gaps that stop larger clients** — effort: weeks; needs: owner decision (write-off policy, statement layouts) and external data (Schedule III wording read from the primary text). There is no write-off or settlement discount, no party-named journals, no sequential voucher numbers, partnership and proprietor statements use the company layout, one revenue account covers all sales, and several Schedule III lines cannot be reached. These matter once the books really move, not before.
22. **Decide and consolidate the practice's own billing and deadline stores** — effort: weeks to months; needs: owner decision (retire the older billing path, retire the older deadline table, maker-checker policy, per-firm payment collection). Two billing systems and two deadline stores are both live, so different screens show different revenue and different overdue lists, and some fee income never reaches the firm's own books or GST return. Choosing the survivor is an owner decision; workflow triggers that really fire, proposals, budgets and maker-checker follow.
23. **Make the code safer to change: real-database tests, typed contract, smaller files, a second maintainer** — effort: months; needs: money (a second engineer or reviewer) and owner decision (move the preview-to-Workers hosting; retire dead routes). About one in five routes has no caller, routers hold the real business logic (payroll alone is 8,162 lines), a second in-memory implementation runs in fast tests and hides production bugs, and one human owner holds the knowledge. This is the slow, expensive part. Start with the second maintainer and real-database tests, because they reduce the risk of everything above.
24. **Independent assurance and the DPDP pack** — effort: weeks to months; needs: money (assessor, counsel) and owner decision (encryption of sensitive columns, who reads the audit log). After the holes are fixed, commission an independent penetration test and keep the summary for buyers. Build read-logging, person-by-person access that reaches reads, a request register and breach clock for the 13 May 2027 duties, and a recovery path for lost two-factor devices. SSO (security_privacy-37) and ISO 27001 or SOC 2 (security_privacy-40) wait for a named buyer who requires them.
25. **Not scheduled for certain in this window: each needs an owner decision, a registration, or a pilot firm to ask for it** — effort: weeks to months each; needs: owner decision, external registration, or pilot demand. Manufacturing, price lists, post-dated cheques, bank feeds through individual banks, e-way bill files, refund workings, TDS for clients whose books are elsewhere, payroll into the employee's own ITR, trusts, foreign-asset schedules, TCS, TRACES, leave management, WhatsApp, and regional languages all have real demand somewhere, but none is on the path to the first referenceable firms. Some appear in Stage 4 or 5 of the roadmap only if a pilot firm asks; the rest stay on the list.

## 6. Moats, as judged after the skeptic pass

- **Referenceable firms in one region** [moderate] — In a small, referral-driven profession a peer's experience travels through study circles and WhatsApp groups faster than any marketing, and each respected reference takes at least one filing season to earn. A funded rival can copy the recruiting playbook in weeks but not the seasons. This is the only moat the skeptics left at moderate on the go-to-market side, and it only exists once real firms run real months on the product. Today there are none.
- **Verified statutory rules, shown with their source and date** [moderate] — Most of the product's rule constants are graded as secondary or unverified, and a wrong figure that a CA quotes in a notice reply would destroy trust. Putting a 'verified as of' stamp, the source document and a public changelog beside each figure, and naming gaps instead of guessing, is a habit incumbents rarely show. The skeptics kept this at moderate but warned it is perishable: every Finance Act resets what verified means, Tally's paid updates service does the same job, and ClearTax has content teams. The edge is candour and discipline, not secrecy.
- **Multi-client payroll for firms that run payroll for many companies** [moderate] — The repo's audit says greytHR, Keka and Zoho Payroll assume one employer running its own payroll, while a CA firm runs payroll for many client companies, month after month. Payroll is monthly and sticky, and employee history plus year-to-date withholding is expensive to rebuild elsewhere. The skeptic rated it moderate, with two cautions: Zoho could add a multi-client view in quarters, and professional tax is modelled for only 4 of about 22 states.
- **One ledger behind every return: tie-out, period lock and acknowledgement capture** [weak] — The product already checks each return against the ledger, locks a period when a return is filed, and names what it cannot compute. Extending that loop to every filing family (GST, ITR, TDS, provident fund, ESIC) by accepting the acknowledgement the CA already downloads, reading the number and writing the lock needs no permission from any government body and no stored credentials. It is a workflow advantage and only exists if the books are kept here. Both skeptic reviews left it weak.
- **A two-way door beside Tally: import in, export out, practice layer on top** [weak] — Every incumbent wants to pull the client into its own ledger, so none can credibly offer a neutral practice layer, and a full export is the strongest lawful answer to a fear of lock-in. But Tally import is already offered by Zoho and others, and a Tally-beside connector already exists at rivals. So this is a door to get through, not a wall. The skeptics kept it weak and warned that a bare journal import will not make GST work because returns read invoices and bills.
- **Consent-ledgered learning per firm, and the client portal as a daily habit** [weak] — A firm's own corrections (which account, which TDS section, which tax treatment it chose for a vendor) and its clients' routine use of the portal are the only things that get more valuable the longer a firm stays. But both need volume (about 7 clients today) and the firm must be able to export them. The skeptics rated per-firm learning weak (copyable in weeks; a retention device) and the portal a catch-up with rivals. Pooling across firms was dropped.
- **A per-client map of what each client has authorised, and until when** [weak] — GST access consents expire and any client can revoke them in one click; income-tax intermediary consents and e-verification windows are the client's clocks. A single screen that shows a 100-client practice which client has authorised what until when, degrades gracefully to manual upload when one lapses, and holds scoped credentials safely could matter to a large practice. The skeptic found no moat: the state belongs to others, rivals can rebuild it by re-consenting clients, and storing credentials makes one breach expose every client.

## 7. What would change this plan

- If the production Gemini key is already on paid no-training terms, the AI confidentiality fix shrinks to a disclosure task and the AI and security scores rise sooner.
- If the Supabase project is already on a plan with daily backups, the operations score in Stage 1 rises and the restore drill moves earlier; if it is on the free plan, this becomes the most urgent item in the plan.
- If real Tally exports from pilot firms tie to the paisa with little work, the migration wall is lower than planned and the pilot cohort can start earlier. If they fail often on custom fields, dates or control characters, the importer timeline doubles and moves past 1 April 2027.
- If pilot firms will not renew at a paid price after a full quarter, the trust and practice scores should not rise past about 6 whatever else is built, and the plan should be rescoped around what the firms actually use.
- If GSTN accepts new GSP applications, or a provider offers white-label terms that do not forbid a competing practice product, the rented-connection route gets cheaper; if provider contracts bar competing practice software, the read-only GST plan and its score must be cut.
- If the new-Act income-tax schemas for tax year 2026-27 are published early, the income-tax file build can finish inside the window; if they appear after mid-2027, the file work slips out of the 18 months.
- If ICAI guidance forbids a member naming a software vendor as a reference, or restricts cloud use of client data, the pilot-reference plan and the data-processing wording change.
- If counsel says PracticeSync becomes a data fiduciary for any pooled or learned data, the per-firm learning and any benchmark plans stay firm-scoped and the data-moat stays weak.
- If Tally, Zoho or TaxOne ship a multi-client cloud practice layer with a neutral import, the 'neutral practice layer' positioning weakens and price and references become the only levers.
- If the measured invoice-reading accuracy on real documents is poor, the AI score stays low and the assistant tools are postponed; if it is good, batch upload and learned defaults move earlier.
- If ICAI's own counts show far fewer firms in the 15 to 150 client band than assumed, the regional ceiling in the reality check should be cut.
