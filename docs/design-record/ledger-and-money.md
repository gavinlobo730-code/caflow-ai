## From CLAUDE.md section: Money and the general ledger

- **A VOUCHER'S LINES HAVE AN ORDER AND TWO FUNCTIONS RECORD IT** (ACC-16, migration
  384). `journal_lines.line_order` is the zero-based position the line held in the
  jsonb array the posting was called with, read out with `WITH ORDINALITY` by
  `post_journal_atomic` AND by `edit_posted_journal` — the second matters because a
  correction DELETEs every line and re-inserts them, so leaving it alone would have
  lost the CA's order the first time they fixed the voucher, silently. Taking the
  order from the array is what let every posting function stay unchanged: a field
  every caller must set is a field some caller will not. **Nothing already posted is
  backfilled** and that is a decision — migration 251 makes a posted line immutable,
  so a backfill would mean disabling that trigger against production for a DISPLAY
  order. An existing line keeps `line_order` NULL and
  `domain/accounting/line_order.py` orders it at read time: debits before credits,
  then `created_at`, then `id`. That chain is TOTAL, which is the property that
  matters — the same voucher renders the same way on every read. **There is
  deliberately no TypeScript mirror**: the one place a CA sees a voucher's lines is
  `GET /api/accounting/journal/{id}`, and the browser's own `journal_lines` embed
  only SUMS debits. A guard fails if `apps/web` ever mentions the column, because
  PostgREST can express neither "debits before credits" nor a fallback chain as an
  `ORDER BY` and the rule would then need mirroring —
  `tests/fixtures/journal_line_order.json` is already the table for it.

- **THE LIVE REPORTS LEAVE AS A SERVER-MADE PDF OR EXCEL THAT IS THE SCREEN, AND A REPORT THAT DOES NOT FOOT IS NOT PRINTED** (accounting-16). Note on ids: accounting-16/20/21/22 are the 30-09-2026 sweep's own ids, NOT the older audit's ACC-16/20/21/22, which already mean other closed findings (line order, a dead route, a posting escaping its client, the ledger drill-through). "Print as PDF" was the browser's print dialog and cash flow exported a CSV, so the ledger, trial balance, cash flow and the two ageing reports, which are exactly what a CA hands a banker or an auditor, had no server document. `GET /api/report-exports/{report}` now makes one. `domain/reporting/export_document.py` and `export_builders.py` turn the dict the screen's OWN endpoint returns into one `ReportDocument` and compute nothing; a ledger whose lines do not carry its opening balance to its closing one, and an ageing whose rows do not add to its total, are REFUSED in words, while a trial balance or cash flow that does not balance prints its verdict as a note (a CA chasing the difference wants the document). `services/report_pdf_service.py` (pdf_style, `numbered()`, `repeatRows`, `domain/money_text`, "Rs." never the rupee glyph) and `report_xlsx_service.py` (exact `Decimal` numbers in the browser exports' own Indian format) are two renderers of that one document; `services/report_export_service.py` calls the functions each screen calls through `routers.accounting._reporting_service(current_user)`, so the caller's client scope is the screen's, and reads the ledger through the paged `account_ledger_page`. **Bounded by measurement**: a PDF is refused past 6,000 ledger lines (ReportLab lays one out at 1.2 to 2.8 ms a line against a worker killed at 120 s; the largest control account measured is 5,659 lines), a spreadsheet past 50,000. It is headed by the PRACTICE (`gstin_of`, BOTH `firms` columns in the projection) and titled with the CLIENT, the opposite of the customer statement's letterhead, because this is a report the practice hands over and not a document the client issues. **The Day Book was deliberately NOT built in this change** (its screen read `journal_entries` straight over PostgREST in the browser and no server function existed to reuse, so a second reader would have let the file and the screen disagree, which is the thing this change is about); it was built on 18-10-2026 once the server day-book function the screen also calls existed (POST-A-091, the reporting record). P&L and Balance Sheet keep their browser Excel and the year-end pack. No migration.

- **INTEREST A CLIENT CHARGES ITS OWN CUSTOMER IS COMPUTED AND PREPARED AS A DRAFT, NEVER POSTED OR INVOICED ON ITS OWN** (accounting-22, migration 461). The product computed statutory interest in many places and could not compute the commercial interest a client charges an overdue customer, so CAs worked it in Excel. `domain/sales/late_interest.py` is the rule and reads nothing: simple interest, actual/365, from the day AFTER the start date (the due date unless the customer's terms say the invoice date) plus grace, up to and including the as-at date, half a paisa and over rounds up ONCE on the whole integer product; the convention is stated on every answer. **The base is `outstanding_paise`**, migration 278's generated column, read and never re-subtracted (a guard fails any subtraction touching a `*_paise` name in the service). **`customers.late_interest_rate_bps` is nullable with no default and NULL is not 0**: no rate on record, a recorded 0 ("waived") and a rate with nothing late are three different answers, and a customer with overdue invoices and no rate is LISTED with the balance and no figure, because "nobody can tell" must not look like "none". **Interest accrues continuously and is billed in pieces**, so `late_interest_charges` records the period each draft covers and the next preview starts the day after what a STANDING draft covers; a charge stands only while its draft does (derived at read time from the invoice's own status, so deleting or cancelling a draft releases the period with no second write), a unique index on (sales_invoice_id, period_to) is the two-clicks backstop, and an interest invoice is never the base of the next one. The draft goes through `routers.sales_invoices.create_invoice` at the GST rate and place of supply of the invoice it relates to (CGST s.15(2)(d), `[S]`: egress is refused here), under a service-kind catalogue item "Interest on delayed payment" (a goods line is how inventory recognises a stock movement), and three refusals are NAMED, never guessed: not an ordinary domestic taxable supply, several GST rates on one invoice, no lines. **Deliberately not done**: nothing is posted, issued, emailed or scheduled; no compounding; no debit-note form (invoice vs debit note and the time of supply are the CA's decisions); and the balance is TODAY's open balance applied to the whole late period, so a part-payment received late OVERSTATES the figure, which every answer says.

- **A POST-DATED CHEQUE IS A MEMORANDUM UNTIL A DUE CHEQUE IS CONVERTED, AND CONVERSION IS AN ORDINARY RECEIPT OR VENDOR PAYMENT** (accounting-21, migration 460). A customer hands over three cheques dated ahead and the product had nowhere to hold them, so a CA either recorded the receipt at once (money in the bank ledger that is not in the bank, an invoice settled by a cheque that has not cleared) or kept a list outside. `post_dated_cheques` has NO journal column; a received cheque names a customer and an issued one a supplier (a CHECK makes the other combinations unrepresentable), and `allocations` are INTENT, not a claim: nothing is reserved against a document and the engine re-validates each against the live outstanding. `domain/banking/pdc.py`: **due is derived, never stored**, and means on or after the cheque's OWN date; stale (three calendar months, `[S]`) is derived too and WARNS, it never refuses, because conversion records a fact and a CA holding proof the bank honoured an old cheque must still be able to record it. Convert calls `receipt_service.create_receipt_core` (received) or `purchase_payment_service.create_payment_core` (issued) and nothing else writes the books (a test reads the service's writes off the AST: its own table only); the receipt is dated the day it was PRESENTED, so the closed-year and filed-return checks are the engine's, at that date. **Conversion is claimed before it is performed**: a compare-and-set held to converted is the first act, so two clicks make one receipt, and a refused engine call puts the cheque back to held. **Two database clients, on purpose**: the register's own table is written by the privileged client (service-role-only by grant) while the engine is handed the REQUEST-scoped client, so row-level security applies as on the Receipts screen. **Deviation from the batch note, deliberate**: it said conversion goes through `bank_posting_service.post`; that path settles an IMPORTED STATEMENT LINE and a PDC has none, so it goes through the two engines `match_and_settle_multi` itself ends in (same journal, same settlement, same validation; only the `bank_transactions` bookkeeping is not shared). **Deliberately not done**: cheque printing (per-bank layouts nobody holds); a cheque that bounces after conversion is reversed on the Receipts or Payments screen like any document, the register does not model a dishonour; customer TDS on a received cheque (`tds_paise` is 0).

- **A PRICE LIST IS A PRE-FILL SOURCE FOR AN INVOICE LINE'S RATE AND NOTHING ELSE** (accounting-20, migration 459). `service_catalogue` carries one selling rate per item, so a trading client quoting dealer, retail and wholesale rates remembered each and typed it onto every line. `price_lists` (owned by ONE client, because a catalogue item is client-owned), `price_list_items` (one strictly positive whole-paise rate per list and item; a composite key makes the database refuse an item filed under the wrong client) and `customers.price_list_id` (nullable, no default, no backfill, ONE writer: `price_list_service.assign`). **It changes no tax and posts nothing, and the invoice keeps whatever rate it was given**: the create path takes the rate from the request and never reads a list, and the rule that keeps it out is stated as a rule and not a list of files, the set of files in `apps/api` that mention a price list at all is exactly the three that own it plus the line that mounts the router. `domain/sales/price_list.resolve` answers in three states that are not interchangeable: the customer's list rate, the catalogue rate WITH the reason it stood in (no list, an archived list, no price for this item), or none with a rate of None and NEVER 0 (the catalogue's own "0 = no default price" is a state, and a pre-filled 0 would be a free line nobody chose). Nothing is computed: no discount, no mark-up. A list is ARCHIVED, not deleted, and a customer on an archived list falls back to the catalogue rate and the answer says so. **In the invoice editor the resolved rate is applied only to the line just picked and only while its rate is still the catalogue pre-fill**, so a rate the CA has typed is never overwritten by a slower answer, a failed answer leaves the catalogue rate, and the save path never asks. **Deliberately not done**: customer GROUPS (the title says so, the detail asks only for a default list per customer); the quotation, credit-note and debit-note editors do not pre-fill from a list; the importers do not set a customer's list.

- **THE INDIAN DAY IS NEVER READ OFF THE UTC CLOCK, AND THE RULE IS A TEST WITH AN EMPTY ALLOWLIST, WHOSE LIMITS ARE WRITTEN DOWN** (POST-A-048 and the sweep it opened, 17-10-2026). Closing POST-A-048 (the overdue-task count used `date.today()`) and asking what else did the same found forty-two places in twenty-three modules (by a count of the diff) that name yesterday between 00:00 and 05:30 IST, the five and a half hours in which the container's UTC clock is a day behind India; on 1 April that is the previous financial year. CLAUDE.md already recorded that an earlier sweep had missed this by searching one spelling. By effect: a **cancellation reversal** of a sales invoice or a purchase bill was dated, and checked against the financial-year lock, a day early (a reversal made at 01:40 IST on 1 April was judged as 31 March, a year that may already be locked), and so was the **bank undo** reversal when it falls back to today because the original period is closed; a **payment** that carried no date, a foreign payment and the **compensating reversal** after a failed settlement were dated a day early; the fallback date of a posted invoice, bill, receipt, note or asset disposal (nine places in `phase2_journal_service`; it applies only when the document carries none) was the UTC day; a **default opening date** (opening balances, trial-balance import) fell in the previous financial year, and so did the movement date of a seeded opening-stock row that carried none; the **ageing** report, the **stock** fallback dates and the **FX** reporting date said yesterday; **numbering** that carries the year (proposals, engagement letters) kept the old year for the first five and a half hours of 1 January; the **overdue-task** count that feeds the executive dashboard and the morning digest did not count a task due the day before, and read at most 1,000 rows (POST-A-048's second half, now `fetch_all`); an **escalation rule** that fires "in exactly N days" measured from yesterday; the **timeline** filed an event under last month on the first of the month; the **automation statistics** compared the first ten characters of a UTC stamp with today, in the repository and in the in-memory twin; the GSTR-3B 4(B)(2) drill-down showed a register row under the UTC day it was recorded. **The fix is one clock.** Every site uses `core.ist_clock.ist_today()` / `ist_now()`, and `ist_date_of(stamp)` is new for the one case where the input is a stored instant (a datetime or an ISO string, naive read as UTC because that is what the database stores; a date passes through; anything unreadable is None): it converts the STAMP and not the question, which CLAUDE.md already said to do and which had no shared helper in `core.ist_clock`. Private copies exist and were left alone (they differ on how a naive value is read, so folding them is not mechanical; ledger POST-A-251). `jobs/claims.ist_date_of` and the recurring-task idempotency check were already right and now delegate to the shared one, so two of the modules touched here are refactors that fixed nothing. **The cancellation month needed more than the date**: GSTR-3B's reversal of credit on a cancelled bill is keyed by the month the bill was cancelled, and that month was read off the UTC `cancelled_at` stamp while the ledger's reversal date became Indian, so one cancellation at 01:40 IST on 1 August could sit in July's return and August's ledger. The return now reads the cancellations from a day earlier in UTC and keeps those whose INDIAN day is inside the period (`gst_return_service._bills_cancelled_in`); the posted-bill read (a bill live at the period's end, cancelled the next Indian day) and the advance-tax allocation day (`gst_advance_service`) do the same, and the memory pipeline buckets by the Indian month. **One edge is deliberately not repaired**: a purchase bill cancelled between 00:00 and 05:30 IST on the first of a month BEFORE this change already has a reversal journal dated the last day of the month before, and the return now declares its cancellation in the new month, so the books-against-ledger comparison disagrees for both months if either return is rebuilt or reopened. Only rows cancelled in that window before deployment are affected; whether to re-date them is a decision for the books, not for a sweep. **A dormant detector came to light and now runs.** `MemoryPipeline.detect_deadline_at_risk` parsed `compliance_records.due_date` (a DATE) to a naive datetime and subtracted it from an aware clock, which raises, and the `except` beside it swallowed the error: it had never produced a trigger. It counts days from the Indian day now, so from the next daily run it creates "Deadline At Risk" triggers (deduplicated over seven days) for open compliance records due within the client's usual response time plus two days; that is what it was written to do, and it is a change in what staff are shown, not only in a date. **The rule** (`tests/test_the_indian_day_is_never_taken_from_the_utc_clock.py`) walks every production module and flags a calendar date, year or month taken from the server's or the UTC clock: `date.today()`, `datetime.today()`, a bare `datetime.now()`, and `.date()`, `.year` / `.month` / `.day`, a `.strftime()` whose format names no time of day, `[:4]` / `[:7]` / `[:10]`, `.split("T")[0]`, or an f-string or `format()` spec naming no time of day, taken from a UTC instant. An instant is `datetime.utcnow()`, `datetime.now(<anything naming utc>)` (positional or `tz=`), a call to a helper that returns one (derived from the code, so a new wrapper is seen), a name held in the SAME function whose every assignment is an instant, or something derived from one by `.isoformat()`, `.replace()`, `.astimezone(utc)`, `str()` or `+`/`-`. It resolves aliased imports (`import datetime as dt`, `from datetime import date as _d`), and a table of 42 cases shows it flags each spelling and does not flag a real instant, `ist_today()` or `ist_now()`. Against the previous tree it reports 37 sites in 20 files. The allowlist is EMPTY and a test asserts it stays so. **Held by a test that fails when the line is reverted**: every fix above that is a behaviour, each with the clock frozen at 20:10 UTC on 31 March (01:40 IST on 1 April), including the two stored-stamp fixes in the GSTR-3B return (a June-dated and a July-dated bill cancelled at that instant), the memory pipeline's month bucket and deadline detector, and the seeded-row date; the reversal and payment-date sites are held by the rule, not by a behavioural test each. **What the negative control shows, exactly.** Of the 90 tests in this change's first two test files, 25 fail against the previous production code: thirteen only because `ist_date_of` does not exist there, and twelve because the behavioural tests freeze the Indian clock in `core.ist_clock`, so the previous modules, which read the real clock, answer a different date. That shows the tests are wired to the helpers; the evidence that the old SPELLINGS are wrong is the rule, which reports them. **What the rule does not see, and where each is recorded.** (1) A stored stamp read off a row and sliced (`row["created_at"][:10]` compared with a date) is a different defect from a clock read, and no rule here can tell a column that holds a date from one that holds an instant: POST-A-251 lists the sites still there; the ones fixed in this change were found by hand. (2) SQL's `CURRENT_DATE` is the database's day, and production's session time zone reads UTC (checked 17-10-2026): `schedule_iii_ageing` (migration 436) uses it only to decide whether to print the "balance outstanding today" caveat, so for those five and a half hours the caveat is wrongly omitted for a report as at yesterday, while its Python twin is now called with the Indian day and does print it; this change opened that difference and POST-A-252 closes it. (3) An instant held in an attribute, a dict, a closure's enclosing function, a module-level constant or a parameter is not followed; nor one reached through `x = x or datetime.now(timezone.utc)`, a conditional expression, a tuple or augmented assignment, a UTC zone held in a constant not named like one, or a two-level wrapper (the list is in the test's header, and none occurs in the production tree). (4) Helpers are derived by NAME across the tree and a name qualifies only if every definition of it returns a UTC instant, so one new `def _now(): return ist_now()` anywhere switches `_now()[:10]` off everywhere; a test exists to notice that. (5) Stored instants themselves (`created_at`, `executed_at`) stay UTC, a date computed from a document's own date was always right, and a screen's own idea of today is the browser's date module with `a-calendar-date-is-never-read-back-in-utc.test.ts`.

- **A STOCK, DISPOSAL OR BANK POSTING ASKS THE FILED-RETURN LOCK WHEN ITS EFFECT REACHES A RETURN, AND THE OTHERS THE LEDGER LISTED STAY OPEN ON PURPOSE, EACH FOR A REASON WRITTEN BESIDE IT** (POST-A-089, 17-10-2026). The ledger called ten dated posting paths "one call in the established shape". They were not: the question per path is the one the receipts argument asks (SALES-15), could this fact change what a filed return said, and the answers differ. **Asked now, by effect and not by a flag:** `fixed_assets.dispose_asset` **when the disposal states a GST rate** (a supply, CGST s.18(6); the journal credits GST Output and `gst_return_service._disposals_declaring_gst` reads exactly the rows with a rate into GSTR-3B Table 3.1(a); a scrapping that states no rate posts no tax and is declared nowhere, so it is not asked); a **stock adjustment that reverses input credit** (a decrease, marked by the CA, on an item with a non-zero rate, which is exactly when `post_stock_writeoff_journal_entry` credits GST Input and registers the s.17(5)(h) reversal GSTR-3B Table 4(B)(1) declares), asked identically by `inventory.adjust_stock` and by the count sheet through `domain/inventory/adjustment_effect`, so one shortage gets one answer on both doors; and `bank_posting_service.post` **when the line is posted with a GST split** (BANK-24: the rate posted is what GSTR-3B Table 4(A) reads; a zero rate or no split declares nothing and is not asked). **The count sheet changed to match:** `post_session` asked the lock unconditionally, which refused the main use of a count sheet, a 31 March stock-take keyed in during April or May after March's returns are filed, for a sheet that declares nothing; it asks only when a line reverses credit now. The bank refusal offers "post it without the GST split" only when the reason is a filed return (a closed year stops the plain post too, and an unverifiable check is no reason to give up the credit) and says that way out does not claim the credit on the line. **The known cost, chosen and written down:** all three are carried by GSTR-3B in this product's builders, but the lock is the combined one (any return covering the date), as `create_asset`'s already is, so from the day GSTR-1 is filed (the 11th) they are refused although the still-open GSTR-3B (the 20th) would carry them, and the message names the GSTR-1. One definition of "filed" for every path is the price; narrowing these three to a filed GSTR-3B means a filing-type argument to the lock and is not done. **Open on purpose** (the reasons are in `tests/test_every_dated_posting_path_asserts_the_client_lock.py::NOT_YET`): re-posting an existing invoice's journal (GSTR-1 is built from the invoice rows, so refusing would leave the ledger without revenue the filed return carries), an NRV write-down (value only), year-end adjustments (posted after the period they adjust, by nature; the route takes any account including the GST ledgers, a true-up of those to the filed returns is the accepted case and a carve-out is undecided), an ordinary bank line (June's reconciliation happens after June's returns, migration 361), an imported trial balance (a migration's opening position) and the AS 11 revaluation (P&L and balance sheet only). `manual_journal_service.update` already asked the same SQL function under another name (`_lock_reason`), which the guard does not recognise by name, so its entry says so. The posting kernel's CLOSURE lock (a locked year, a finalised client year) reaches every one of them either way. A side find: `tests/test_fixed_asset_disposal.py` assigned a stub onto the shared journal service and never restored it, so the output-tax disposal tests failed whenever the two ran in that order in one process; an autouse fixture restores it now. NOT DECIDED, and still on the list: the purchase payments (a payment can carry TDS, which does land in a return) and the GST return rows themselves (`save_gstr1`, `save_gstr3b`, `save_gstr9`).

- **A RANGE OF DAYS IS A RANGE OF INDIAN DAYS, A STORED STAMP IS SHOWN AS THE INDIAN DAY IT FELL ON, AND THE AGEING NOTE'S "TODAY" IS THE INDIAN ONE** (POST-A-251, POST-A-252, 18-10-2026; migration 514). **(1) The ageing caveat.** `public.schedule_iii_ageing` printed "Amounts are each document's balance outstanding TODAY ... A document settled between that date and today is not included" when `p_as_of < CURRENT_DATE`, and `CURRENT_DATE` is the date in the SESSION's time zone, which on the hosting platform is UTC, so between 00:00 and 05:30 IST a schedule as at the previous Indian day carried no caveat although a document settled since is missing from it, while the Python twin (given the Indian day) did print it. Migration 514 replaces the condition with `(now() AT TIME ZONE 'Asia/Kolkata')::date`. It is derived from the migration that LAST defined the function (436, found by number), every other line carried forward, and `test_514_...pg.py` reconstructs that ancestor from the migration directory and fails if anything but the condition and the closing comment differs. **The proof does not depend on the hour it runs:** it sets the session time zone to UTC-12 and to UTC+14 and asks for the schedule as at the previous Indian day, the Indian day and the next, because at any instant at least one of the two zones has a calendar date other than the Indian day, so the old `CURRENT_DATE` fails at every hour and not only in the five and a half hours the defect shows; its rollback is the negative control. The parity suite's `_db_today` now asks the database for the Indian day. Searched in the same pass: every other `CURRENT_DATE`, `now()::date` and `CURRENT_TIMESTAMP` in migration function bodies (the only hits are the same condition in 303 and 305, superseded, and 333's check that a date of birth is before today, which is not a statutory day comparison). **(2) The time-entry ranges.** A `timestamptz` comes back as UTC and its first ten characters are the UTC day. The time-entry list, the seven time-analytics reads and the three completed-in-the-period reads of the analytics router handed the database `started_at >= 'YYYY-MM-DDT00:00:00Z'` and `<= 'YYYY-MM-DDT23:59:59Z'`, so an entry started at 01:40 IST on 1 April (31 March 20:10 UTC) was counted in March and the last second of every day was dropped; the mock paths cut `started_at[:10]` to the same effect. `core.ist_clock.ist_day_window(from, to)` gives midnight IST to midnight IST with the end EXCLUSIVE (`.gte` and `.lt`), written with the `+05:30` offset so the database does the conversion; `in_ist_window` is its in-memory twin; `ist_day_iso` is the display form (the time export's date column, the bank reconciliation's completed and reopened dates, the year-end pack's approval history) and an open risk's age is measured in `ist_date_of` days. **(3) The rule.** `tests/test_the_indian_day_is_never_taken_from_the_utc_clock.py` catches a calendar date taken from a clock read and cannot see `row["created_at"][:10]`, because the same slice is right for a date column. The new test finds the shape from the AST: a `[:10]` slice of a value that denotes a `..._at` key (a `get`, a subscript, `str(...)` of either, an `or` fallback, a name or an attribute), over the whole API with a frozen list that may only shrink and goes stale-red when its entry stops matching. A list cut such as `sorted(..., key=lambda a: a["created_at"])[:10]`, another width and a due date are not flagged (negative controls). **Its named blind spots:** a stamp copied into a variable with another name, `.split("T")[0]`, a `strftime` of an unconverted datetime, and a stamp column not named `..._at`. **Left alone, and why:** `intelligence_service.compute_compliance_risk` slices `filed_at`, a column `compliance_records` does not have (the table has `filed_date`), so its late-history rule never runs; making it live changes every client's risk score, so it is ledgered (POST-A-261) and frozen on the list; the four private copies of the stamp-to-day conversion read a naive value differently from one another and stay a decision per copy (POST-A-251, now smaller). **Behaviour that changes on screens:** a monthly time report, a team analytics window and an export column move an entry started between 18:30 and 24:00 UTC to the Indian day it fell on, and an upper bound is now midnight rather than 23:59:59. **Verified:** 22 new Python tests (the three helpers at the 20:10 UTC / 31 March boundary and at the 18:29:59 UTC end of a month; each of the seven analytics reads handing the database `+05:30` bounds with `.lt` and no `.lte`; the in-memory twin counting the same entries as the database would; the entry list; the router's three reads; the export; the risk's age; the rule and its negative controls; against the previous source 12 of the 22 fail, the other ten being helper unit tests), 13 tests for 514, 3 of them without a database (the derivation, the absence of a session clock and the rollback's text) and 10 against real Postgres (both zones and three days, STABLE and SECURITY DEFINER and the grants unchanged, idempotent, and the rollback showing the old behaviour disagree with the Indian day in at least one zone) and the existing parity suite (40) against the changed function. **Not verified:** the time reports were not driven in a browser, and no PDF was rendered with an approval stamp in the 18:30 to 24:00 UTC window. **THE RULE'S KEYS ARE THE SCHEMA'S (POST-A-251, 31-10-2026).** The `[:10]` rule judged a value by the `..._at` suffix alone, and nine of production's timestamptz columns are not named that way (`ack_date`, `ewb_date`, `ewb_valid_upto` and six more). Its stamp names are now `..._at` PLUS every column the schema snapshot types timestamptz wherever it appears, so a new such column is covered by refreshing the snapshot. The three sites it then found had an ambiguity no reader could settle (Indian wall-clock labelled UTC, or a true instant): the e-way bill and e-invoice doors now store the day the portal printed, as a bare date, through `core.ist_clock.portal_day` (no offset is Indian wall-clock; an offset is converted; text that is not a date is a 422), and the readers use `ist_date_of` / `ist_day_iso`, which agree with a day stored as midnight UTC. Production holds one such value (an acknowledgement date at 10:30 UTC, the same day either way) and no e-way bill dates. Still a blind spot: a stamp copied into a variable with another name before it is sliced.

- **A YEAR-END IS FINALISED BY CLOSING THE CLIENT'S YEAR FIRST AND MARKING THE ENGAGEMENT LOCKED SECOND, AND THE REOPEN TAKES THE OPPOSITE ORDER, SO A FAILURE BETWEEN THE TWO LEAVES THE BOOKS CLOSED** (POST-A-315, 22-10-2026).
